Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my freelance day rate?

Start with your target take-home, gross it up for tax, add expenses, then divide by your billable days. Billable days = working days − holidays − sick/admin days, × your billable utilisation rate. This calculator does all of that automatically.

What is a typical freelance day rate in the UK?

UK day rates vary hugely by sector: software developers £400–£800/day, digital marketers £250–£500/day, UX designers £350–£650/day, copywriters £200–£450/day, project managers £350–£600/day. Senior and specialised contractors command premium rates.

Should I charge the same as my permanent equivalent?

No — as a freelancer you pay your own tax, NI, pension, and have no holiday or sick pay. Your day rate should typically be 1.5–2× a comparable permanent salary equivalent to break even. Use this calculator to find your personal break-even.

What expenses can I offset as a freelancer?

Common deductible expenses: home office costs, equipment, software subscriptions, professional development, travel, accountant fees, professional indemnity insurance, and marketing costs. Keep receipts for everything.

How much should I set aside for tax?

UK self-employed: set aside ~25–30% of invoices to cover income tax and Class 4 NI. US self-employed: set aside ~25–30% for federal/state income tax and 15.3% self-employment tax. Many freelancers run a separate tax savings account.

What is the Freelancer Rate Calculator?

A freelancer rate calculator works out the day or hourly rate you need to charge to hit your target income after tax, expenses and non-billable time. This free freelance rate calculator turns a take-home goal into a rate you can quote with confidence.

How it works

Start from your target take-home pay, then gross it up for tax and add business costs. Divide by the days you can actually bill: day rate = (target income + expenses) ÷ billable days. Of ~230 working days a year, only around 150–180 are usually billable after holidays, admin and marketing.

Freelancers also carry tax, National Insurance / self-employment tax and no paid leave, which is why a healthy day rate is typically 1.5–2× a comparable permanent salary.

From salary to day rateYou want the equivalent of a £50,000 salary. Add ~30% for tax/NI and no benefits (≈£65,000) plus £5,000 expenses = £70,000. Divide by 160 billable days ≈ £438/day.
Hourly from a day rateIf your target day rate is £480 and you bill 7 hours a day, your hourly rate is £480 ÷ 7 ≈ £69/hour.

When to use it

  • Setting your first freelance rate after going independent.
  • Deciding whether a client's budget is worth taking.
  • Reviewing rates annually to keep pace with costs.
  • Comparing a contract offer against a permanent salary.
